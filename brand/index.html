The marks

Three versions, all in SVG, all locked to the same geometry. Red for most things, one colour where you only get one, reversed where the background is already red. No size grades. Use whatever size it lands at.

The specimen sheets

Six sheets the Office keeps on its own desk. They are photographs of the marks in use — on paper, on cloth, on a jersey, on a bidon. They are reference, not instruction. You do not have to reproduce these. That is rather the point of them.

Two things the Office would like you to know

The mark is a picture and nothing else. There is no text inside the circle — no Latin letters, no characters of any alphabet, ever. Not because Latin is the proper language for a bird, but because we tried it with the other one and it kept getting the name wrong. The word appeared as four different wrong birds in one afternoon. A mark gets copied a hundred times and the mistake gets copied a hundred times with it.

The bird has no pouch. It has a beak like a thorn and a body that is more convincing than a real pelican's. Two realistic versions were made and both were thrown away. A mark does not need to be a good drawing of the thing. It needs to be a good drawing of this thing.
