# 作品集海報（每個作品風格不同）

> 檔名沿用先前的 slop.md，但「slop」是誤稱——這些是有品味的小作品。

與 golden-pelican 系列的「案件卷宗 / 目擊現場」風格刻意區隔：
這裡每個作品用**它自己的調性**，不做統一視覺系統。

原則沿用已驗證的做法：
- 資訊文字用中文或英文都可以，但**逐字列出**並要求不得出現其他文字
- 主體是**照片**或**平面物件**，不是白紙上排版
- 輸出 3:4（1152×1536）

## BV-01｜線上打小人出氣筒 · 祭壇主場景

```
A photograph of a small paper altar at night, shot on a phone with slight
motion blur, as if the person holding it had just swung something. The altar is
a folding table covered in dark red and gold paper, creased and worn. Two
candles burn at the left and right edges, their flames streaked by the motion
blur. In the centre stands a flat cut-out paper figure of a stick-thin office
worker, already torn at one shoulder, a smear of red ink across the chest. A
rubber slipper lies in front of the altar, mid-air, dropped. Rising smoke
curls up through the frame. The room behind is dark and cluttered with
newspaper.

Printed on a paper banner taped across the top of the altar in brush
calligraphy, the characters large and confident:

拖鞋一拍，晦氣消散

Below the altar, in small stamped type:

12 個預設小人 · 4 種法器 · 20 句咒語

No other text anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## BV-02｜線上打小人出氣筒 · 法器與傷害值

```
A photograph of four objects laid out on a dark red cloth, lit from one side by
a single candle so that half of them fall into deep shadow. From left to
right: a cheap blue plastic slipper, an extra-long steel sewing needle, a
nine-section metal whip, and a wooden sword carved with faded characters. Each
one is worn, chipped or scratched. A small red paper card stands behind each
object, and a thin trail of smoke passes over all of them.

Each red card is printed with a single large number, and the numbers increase

-1    -2    -3    -5

Below them, in a single stamped line:

怒氣集滿 100，桃木劍連環五擊

The leftmost slipper is the cleanest and the rightmost sword is the most
worn, as if the sword is the one that has been used most. No other text
anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## UP-01｜神經痛就醫導航 · 症狀地圖

```
A photograph of a wall-mounted clinic wayfinding sign, matte white vinyl on a
concrete wall, shot straight on under even fluorescent light. The sign is
large and clinical, screwed to the wall at four corners, one corner slightly
lifted. A scuff mark runs across the lower edge. Everything is calm, cold and

The sign carries a clean grid of small pictograms, each in its own box, in a

And at the bottom, smaller:

神經內科 · 復健科

The sign is legible, calm, and slightly worn, like something in a hospital
```

## TT-01｜真話翻譯機 · 同一句話的五種譯法

```
A photograph of a scruffy kitchen table at night, shot from directly above
under a single warm desk lamp hanging low, so the light is a bright pool in the
centre and the edges of the table fall into shadow. The table is not tidy: a
half-empty mug, an open notebook with a pen thrown across it, a crumpled receipt
from a convenience store, and a plate with one cold piece of toast on it, all
pushed to the edges to clear the middle.

Fanned out across the middle of the table are five torn slips of the same lined
paper, all cut from one sheet, all the same width, overlapping slightly like a
hand of playing cards. Each slip carries the same single sentence translated,
and each is written in a completely different hand and a different mood. The
first is small and neat and calm. The second is small and underlined twice. The
third is written fast and slanting. The fourth is in thick marker and slightly
joking. The fifth, at the near edge and closest to the camera, is in a red
marker that has been pressed so hard the strokes have torn through the paper
and dented the table underneath, and the letters are uneven and too big and
have run past the edge of the slip. A small drop of the same red ink has splashed
onto the table beside it.

The five slips read, from the far one to the near one:

不會過，但會拖得很有禮貌。

人類又在加密通訊。

講很多，重點是：先別罵我。

文字越厚，通常是底氣越薄。

猜錯是你誤會，猜對是你懂事。

The fifth slip and its splattered ink are the loudest thing in the frame. The
red ink is the only strong colour in an otherwise warm brown and cream scene.
No other text anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## TT-02｜真話翻譯機 · 請先深呼吸

```
A photograph of a cheap plastic desk fan running on a table in a dim office at
night, shot slightly from below and to one side, as if a person were sitting in
the chair it is pointed at. The fan is off and the blades are perfectly still.
The only light is the blue-white glow of a monitor off to the right, out of
frame, and it lights the fan and one corner of the desk and leaves the rest of
the room dark. A half-drunk cup of tea sweats on the desk, and a stack of
papers sits at the edge of the pool of light.

Propped against the base of the fan, at a slight lean as if just placed there,
is a small folded paper card, printed, creased once down the middle. The card is
the brightest thing in the frame and it is angled so that it faces the empty
chair, as if it is meant to be read by whoever sits down next.

The card reads, in a plain printed face:

貼出去之前

請先深呼吸

Below that, in a much smaller line, printed in grey:

它不會替你判斷人心。它只會拆話術。

The stillness of the stopped fan and the abandoned tea suggest someone who just
got up and walked away. The card is left behind for them. No other text
anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

```
A photograph of a listening station in a dark room: a shelf of five objects,
each one a different piece of discarded studio equipment, lit only by a small
clipped lamp above the middle of the shelf. From left to right: a battered

1 PROMPT
2 TRACK
3 MISREAD
4 RECORD
5 LATER

The pencil lines under three of the five labels have been gone over twice, as
if the writer changed their mind and pressed harder. No other text anywhere. No
watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## PS-02｜THE PROMPT SANG ITSELF · 指令變成樂譜

```
A photograph of a single page of music manuscript lying on a dark wooden desk,
shot from directly above under a warm lamp. The page is cream and slightly foxed
at the edges. On it, printed stave notation in black ink, a short melody in
four bars. But the stave has been overprinted: across the printed notes, in
blurred black as if printed by a machine reading a text field, there is a line
of English text set on the staff itself, sitting where lyrics would normally
go. The two overlap and neither one fully wins.

The overlaid text on the staff reads exactly this:

sorry, I think this part is yours now

The staves are unmarked otherwise. A pencil lies at the bottom edge of the
frame, and a crumb of eraser beside it, as if someone tried to take the words
out and gave up halfway. Warm, quiet, and slightly wrong. No other text
anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## WC-01｜Walk Me There · 貓頭鷹與路口

```
A photograph taken at a real street corner at dusk, from the pedestrian's own
point of view, as if standing there about to be uncertain. A narrow pavement,
a kerb, a crossing with worn paint, a street sign seen from below at an angle
so its lettering is hard to read. The light is that flat blue-grey just after
sunset. A takeaway cup and a crushed can sit on the kerb.

Perched on top of the street sign, small in the frame and lit from the side, is
a small owl. It is not a cartoon: it has real feathers and real talons, and it
is looking directly down the lens, at the person holding the camera, rather than
along the road. Its head is tilted very slightly, the way an owl does when it
is listening rather than deciding. The owl is the only warm thing in the
picture.

The only text in the image is on the street sign, and it is read from below at
an angle, so the letters are foreshortened and only partly legible:

ONE WAY

No other text anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## DO-01｜Draw One · 抽籤

```
A photograph of a plain off-white sheet of thick paper on a matte black surface,
shot from directly above under soft even light with no visible lamp and almost no
shadow. The paper is a warm off-white, the colour of uncoated book paper, with
fine fibre flecks in it and a soft deckled edge on one side. The black surface
under it is a very dark neutral, not a colour, and only a narrow band of it is
visible around the paper. The whole frame is extremely spare: one sheet of
paper, and nothing else except the paper.

The sheet is the page of a plain book. At the top, set in a large old-style
serif typeface with generous letter spacing, is a single line:

數位籤詩宇宙

Below it, in the same serif at a much smaller size, one short line:

今天想問什麼

Below that, ruled across the width of the page, is a single empty ruled box, the
kind you would write in, and the box is empty. A pencil rests on the page at an
angle, its point touching the left edge of the empty box, as if someone had put
it down and then thought about it for a while and not written anything yet. There
is a faint graphite smudge beside the point, where the pencil has been held in
place.

The page is otherwise completely blank. The serif type, the near-black
background, the paper colour and the total absence of decoration are the entire
point. No other text anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## DO-02｜Draw One · 抽出來

```
A photograph of a person's hand holding a single narrow slip of thick off-white
paper, drawn halfway out of a plain dark box, shot close from slightly above at
an angle so the hand is large in the frame. The lighting is soft and neutral,
even and shadowless, the light of a page being read rather than a lamp on a
desk. The skin is lit plainly and there is no dramatic shadow anywhere.

The slip is the same uncoated off-white paper as the box, torn off a long strip,
and the tear is clean and fibrous. The hand holds the slip between thumb and
forefinger, relaxed, and the hand is a real working hand with slightly uneven
nails and a bit of dry skin at the knuckle, not a posed or manicured hand. Only
the hand and the lower part of the box are in the frame; the person's face and
body are not visible.

On the slip, set in a small old-style serif typeface and clearly legible, is a
single column of printed traditional characters, evenly spaced, the way a
numbered poem is set on a temple fortune slip. The characters are in dark ink
on the pale paper and they are the only writing on the slip.

At the bottom of the frame, just visible and slightly out of focus, is the open
box, which is plain and undecorated, with a few more slips standing upright
inside it.

The slip being drawn out of the box, halfway, is the whole action of the image:
one question asked, and one answer not yet read. No other text anywhere. No
watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## RC-01｜Reel Crew · latent film library

```
A photograph of a film archive room, shot down the middle of a long aisle. Metal
shelving runs away from the camera on both sides, stacked to the top with
labelled archive boxes of uniform size, some slightly pulled out. The aisle is
lit by a single strip of fluorescent light running along the ceiling, so the
light falls in a hard line down the middle of the floor and the shelves at the
edges go into darkness. The floor is bare concrete with a painted yellow
guideline running along it, worn and crossed by trolley marks. A rolling
library ladder stands half-way down the aisle, empty.

Every box is labelled with a printed index label of exactly the same size and
same layout, in the same position, on every single box, going as far as the
light reaches. The labels are all identical and none of them can be read at
this distance. A few boxes have been re-labelled, and the newer labels sit
slightly crooked over the old ones.

The point is the sheer number of identical boxes. No other text is legible
anywhere. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```

## CR-02｜奧斯卡片單雷達 · 片名還沒有答案

```
A photograph of a wall covered in small printed index cards, shot straight on
under flat daylight, the way a records office or a language-department notice
board looks. The cards are uniform manila, the same size, each in its own
slot, edge to edge across the whole wall, and there are far too many for the
slots — a second and third layer of cards have been taped flat over the gaps,
so the wall is uneven and some cards sit proud of the rest by a few
millimetres.

Every card is printed in the same layout with a film title in the middle. The
titles are in different scripts and different alphabets: Latin, Cyrillic, Han
characters, and Japanese kana. Nearly all of them have been struck through
with a single thin diagonal line in blue pen, and beside each one a different
second title has been written small in the margin — a different language each
time. Some cards have three candidate titles written around the edge. The
wall reads as an unresolved argument rather than a finished list.

In the middle of the wall, one card has not been struck through. It is the
only card with a red border, and it is pinned slightly crooked. It carries one
title and nothing else, and the pin through it has been pushed in hard enough
to dimple the card:

暫譯

A single pencil hangs on a string at the height of that card. Nothing else on
the wall is legible. No watermark, no logo, no website.
```
