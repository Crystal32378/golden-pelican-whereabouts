First batch — four people, four workshops

Nobody authorised these. Four people saw the bird, or saw a photograph of it, and each of them went to a different workshop and asked for something. They did not know about each other. The Office did not commission a single one of them.

You will notice they do not agree. None of them carries the mark the Office actually uses — the same red roundel you see on the main site and the posters. All four were drawn from memory, from a photograph, or from nothing but the story, and they have come out slightly different, which is correct. The woven label has six spokes and the bird is crooked; the 3D print has a beak that is too long; the sketchbook has a photograph of the actual places pasted on by hand. Nobody checked. All four are red, all four are old cloth or old paper or old plastic, and all four are about a case number.

This is deliberate. Everyone has seen it; everyone draws it slightly differently. The version where all four match is the version where somebody is in charge, and somebody being in charge is the opposite of this page.

How most of it is sold — you pay first, then it is made. Each maker needs a minimum order before the workshop will start, so a single label is not a purchase, it is a contribution toward MOQ 200 BY THE METRE. When the minimum is reached the maker runs the batch; if it is not reached in 60 DAYS the money is returned and nobody loses anything but an afternoon.

One of them is not a batch. The 3D-printed pelican is made after you pay and shipped on its own, so its minimum is MOQ 1 — one bird, one spool of filament, no waiting for anybody else to agree.

None of them has the mark. The roundel the Office uses everywhere else is not on any of these. The bird on all four was drawn by somebody who had only seen it once.

Second batch — made after the mark got out

Eight more, and these are different. All eight carry the mark. Somebody had been circulating the brand manual — the roundel, the rules, the six specimen sheets — and by the time this batch was made it was in circulation. The first four drew the bird from memory and got the spokes wrong; these were working from the file.

Two of them duplicate earlier work. There is now a second notebook with a photograph glued to the front, and a second set of postcards. Two cities, two makers, no correspondence between them. The Office has checked; neither has heard of the other, and both are correct. It has decided not to merge them.

They are not better. They are more consistent, which is a different thing, and it is the thing this page is not for. A bird drawn slightly wrong by somebody who saw it once is evidence. A bird reproduced exactly, from a document, is a print run.

Same terms as the first four. You pay first, then it is made. Each maker needs a minimum before the workshop will start, and if that minimum is not reached in 60 DAYS the money is returned and nobody loses anything but an afternoon.

Two of these have no minimum worth the name. The desk diorama is MOQ 1 — built after you pay, shipped on its own. The snow globe is MOQ 12, because that is how many blanks the man in the shop had.

Priced in US dollars throughout, on both batches, as quoted by the makers. The Office does not convert and does not take a cut, which is stated here only because people keep asking where the money goes.

There is no checkout on this page. Nothing here can be bought. The prices are the prices the workshops quoted, and the progress bars are the Office's honest count of who has put money in — but no money moves, because the Office has no authority to move it. This is a case file, not a shop.
